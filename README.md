# Project Victoria

### Trajectory Based Instability Detection for Young High Risk Cardiac and Autoimmune Patients in the Emergency Department

**Research Area:** Emergency Medicine | Clinical Decision Support | Predictive Analytics

**Project Status:** Research Proposal and Conceptual Development

## Overview

Project Victoria is a proposed clinical decision support framework aimed at improving the early recognition of physiological deterioration in younger patients presenting to the emergency department with cardiac or autoimmune conditions.

Conventional monitoring systems often rely on fixed physiological thresholds to identify clinical instability. However, younger patients may maintain vital signs within accepted ranges even as their physiological condition begins to deteriorate. This raises the possibility that clinically important changes may be overlooked when individual measurements are interpreted without considering their progression over time.

Project Victoria proposes an alternative approach that focuses on how a patient's physiological measurements change relative to their own initial baseline.

## Proposed Methodology

The framework would establish an individualized physiological baseline during the first 60 to 90 minutes of emergency department monitoring.

Rather than evaluating individual vital signs in isolation, the proposed system would examine patterns of physiological change across multiple signals, including heart rate, blood pressure, respiratory rate, and oxygen saturation.

These changes would be integrated into a divergence risk score intended to identify emerging physiological instability, even when individual measurements remain within conventional clinical thresholds.

To minimize alarm fatigue, the framework would prioritize clinically meaningful patterns of deterioration and communicate them through consolidated alerts for senior clinician assessment.

## Research Objectives

1. Investigate whether individualized physiological trajectories can improve the early identification of clinical deterioration.
2. Explore methods for integrating multiple physiological signals into an interpretable divergence risk score.
3. Examine strategies for reducing unnecessary clinical alerts.
4. Develop a validation framework using retrospective data, simulation, and prospective observational evaluation.

## Proposed Research Phases

**Phase 1: Retrospective Analysis**

Examine historical physiological monitoring data to identify patterns preceding documented clinical deterioration.

**Phase 2: Computational Simulation**

Develop and evaluate a preliminary trajectory analysis framework using simulated physiological data.

**Phase 3: Prospective Validation**

If preliminary findings support feasibility, investigate the framework's performance in a clinical environment under appropriate institutional and ethics approvals.

## Current Status

Project Victoria remains in the research proposal stage. The framework has not yet undergone computational or clinical validation, and its proposed benefits remain hypotheses requiring investigation.

## Future Directions

Future development may involve implementing a computational prototype, evaluating different approaches to physiological trajectory analysis, and exploring the feasibility of integrating the framework into existing emergency department monitoring workflows.
